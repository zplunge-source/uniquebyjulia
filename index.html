The form

A knot is the only fastening.

The musubi, or knot bag, has been carried in Japan for centuries: two unequal handles, the long one drawn through the short one, closing the bag by tension alone. Nothing to break, nothing to replace.

Julia keeps that structure exactly and changes everything else — the drape, the weight of the cloth, the way a heavy linen slumps where a taut silk stands. Each piece is cut and finished by hand, twelve to an edition, and then the pattern is retired.

The maker

Julia works alone, and slowly.

She trained in leatherwork, then spent six years cutting patterns for a house that made forty thousand bags a season. She left to make twelve.

Every piece begins as a paper pattern on the studio floor, adjusted for the specific cloth — silk needs a wider handle, calfskin almost none. She cuts, stitches and finishes each bag herself, and signs the inside of the seam.

When an edition sells out, the pattern goes in a drawer and is not used again.

A knot is an honest closure. It either holds or it doesn't, and you can see which.Julia
